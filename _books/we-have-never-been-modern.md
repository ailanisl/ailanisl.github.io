We hate what we can't understand. I think this is true within the sciences, the arts, the mystics, the academics, every cloistered group that may claim their uncloisteredness. 

To Latour, time is not a laminary flow (which is the modern view) but a turbulent whirlpooling. Time is how we sort things. Time: beings and their relationships, networks that construct irreversibility and reversibility. 

> How can we move from transcendent/immanent Nature to a nature that is still as real, but extracted from the scientific laboratory and then transformed into external reality? (77)

> The moderns are quite right to want reality, language, society, and being all at once. They are wrong only in believing that these sets are forever contradictory (89).

This is the joy of reading theoretical works – it feels as if you are expereincing the world theough different sensory organs. A kind of secret that many others do not have yet (and that you did not have a little while ago) and want to expound to all. 

The 4 camps 
1. _Scientists_: for whom Nature is supreme, transcendent, pregiven
2. _Sociologists_: who hold social relations, humans-among-themselves, collectives as ultimately real
3. _Linguists_: everything is text, discourse, sign and sign system
4. _Constructionists_: Everything is constructs, no Being

No. 1 has outpaced the others in this era. Much of the others have self-consciously absorbed the ridicule of materialists or else faded to obscurity. 
Yet, it is only one way of knowing and thus quite limiting. One camp's rules do not have to contradict all others. All are coextensive and contiguous: Latour's Middle Kingdom. 


We moderns confuse __products with processes__:  
> How could we be chilled by the cold breath of the sciences, when the sciences are hot and fragile, human and controversial, full of thinking reeds and of subjects who are themselves inhabited by things?

Everything exists in networks. Networks, defined by bumbling and brilliant humans, our collectives, that foster of intersubjective understanding, and our means of measurement. 
> Try to verify the tiniest fact, the most trivial law, the humblest constant, without subscribing to the multiple metrological networks, to laboratories and instruments.  

We are nothing without our tools (which are hybrids or quasi-objects). Tools are our mediators. Science cannot speak to universals because it relies on human interpretation through measurement.
