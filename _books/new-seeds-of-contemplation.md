## On Contemplation
_contemplation_: to know beyond knowing or "unknowing" 
- Beyond objects, explanations, discourse, dialogue, self

Contemplation cannot be taught or clearly explained: 
> The more objectively and scientifically one tried to analyze it, the more he empties it of its real content... beyond verbalization or rationallization. 

- There is no adequate psychology of contemplation. 

He makes a good point against the atheist's attack on religion for its opiate qualities: 
> Contemplation is far from the ersatz escape from external self to be lost in collective enthusiasm (violence carried out in the name of class, party, nation, race, or sect).

> The false mysticism of the Hass society captivates men who are so alienated from themselves and from God that they are no longer capable of genuine spiritual experience.

Important distinctions: 
- Contemplation ≠ spiritual complacency to the status quo or prejudiced opinions. 
- Detachment ≠ detaching ourselves from things in order to attach to God 
    - Detachment = becoming detached from ourselves to see + use all things in and for God 

It's baby to defile the 'things' as evils (just shows the extend to which we are attached to the presumably 'evil' things)

> In trying to believe in their ego as something "holy," these fanatics look upon everything as unholy.

This should be said about the majority of American religiouis extremeists today (those whose religious dogmatics of unholiness bleed into policial enforcement). Also, religious terrorists. 

## On writing
> If a writer is so cautious that he never writes anything that cannot be criticized, he will never write anything that can be read. If you want to help other people you have got to make up your mind to write things that some men will condemn. 

## On Humility
> A man who is truly humble cannot despair, because in the humble man there is no longer any such thing as self-pity.

The biggest illusion in spiritual life: 
- the false pride to desire the perfection of contemplation –– the summit of mystical union with God... one could argue it doesn't exist, conventionally speaking.

> for the only way to enter into that joy is to dwindle down to a vanishing point and become absorbed in God through the center of your nothingness.

Christian religious self-help is so accusatory, guilts us into becoming better people. 

> The humble man is not disturbed by praise. Since he is no longer concerned with himself... he does not refuse praise.... in receiving it he keeps nothing for himself but gives it all, with great joy, to God....
> The humble man receives praise the way a clean window takes the light of the sun. The true and more intense the light it, the less you see of the glass. 

Merton's writing on humility rings true. When you refuse praise clumsily and awkwardly, you end up bringing even more attention to yourself, even if the intention was the opposite. It shows the opaqueness of your sense of self, as you are insecure about the progress of your being (which in only an illusion to begin with.)

But don't try to be humble (with the lessons learned from this book!). Stop it.

Humility is a paradox! Just as nirvana is, just as nonduality is. The more you talk about it, refuse it, retain it in your mind, you are more the vainer. Anything that involves the mind, consciousness, or self-consciousness, inevitably reduces (and expands) to paradox.
