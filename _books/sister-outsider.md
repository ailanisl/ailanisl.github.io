> As we come more into touch with our own ancient, non-european consciousness of living as a situation to be experienced and interacted with, we learn more and more to cherish our feelings, and to respect those hidden sources of our power from where true knowledge and, therefore, lasting action comes.

> At this point in time, I believe that women carry within ourselves the possibility for fusion of these two approaches so necessary for survival, and we come closest to this combination in our poetry. I speak here of poetry as a revelatory distillation of experience, not the sterile word play that, too often, the white fathers distorted the word poetry to mean - in order to cover a desperate wish for imagination without insight.

"Uses of the Erotic" 
> We have attempted to separate the spiritual and the erotic, thereby reducing the spiritual to a world of flattened affect, a world of the ascetic who aspires to feel nothing. But nothing is farther from the truth... The severe abstinence of the ascestic bbecomes the ruling obsession... Beyond the superficial, the considered phrase, 'It feels right to me,' acknolwedges the strength of the erotic into a true philosophy, for what that means is the first and most powerful guiding light toward any understanding. And understanding is the handmaiden which can only wait upon, or clarify, that knowledge, deeply born. The erotic is the nurturer or nursemain of all our deepest knowledge.

> This is one reason why the erotic is so feared and so often neglected to the bedroom alone, when recognized at all [used as a weapon that constructs inferior hierarchy]. For once we begin to feel deeply in all aspects of our lives, we begin to demand from ourselves and from our life-pursuits that they feel in accordance with that joy which we know ourslves to be capable of.
