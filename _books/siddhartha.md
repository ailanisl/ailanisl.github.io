It's my third re-read. Once in junior year of high school, another time in community college English, and once more for writing my research paper. 

The last time around I read it in a Buddhist modernist lens. It really isn't about Buddhism as much as it's about Protestantism - it kind of resembles the Theosophical movements in the West. Basically, it's Hesse's spirituality veiled as some esoteric Oriental spirituality. I liked the first half, (after reading it the second time) the ending is not Buddhist at all. It reifies the eternal unity and essentializes Being. So, it resembles Brahmanism/Vedic philosophy, which Buddhism was a reaction against (although, this unifying narrative does show up again and again within Buddhist schools).

SO, _Siddhartha_ is everything _but_ Buddhism. Yet, in saying so, it is completely and totally Buddhist! 

Idk.
