BIO

I graduated from UC Berkeley with a bachelor's in cognitive science and a minor in Buddhist studies. There, I did some independent (idiosyncratic) research on how conceptual metaphor may have accounted for the distinctly modern and individualistic model of mind in American Buddhism, which pervades current mindfulness movements and neuroscience research of meditation.

I also did some lab and research apprenticeships during my time at Berkeley. With the MetaNet Project , I studied how far-right groups reasoned about climate change through religious metaphor (e.g., altar of climate, St. Greta) with Eve Sweetser and Kim Grogan (UBC). I also helped out at Berkeley's Language and Cognitive Development Lab, where we looked at how children determine group belonging based on language cues and how Hindu and Muslim children in Gujarat, India thought about norms, violations, and loopholes of religious groups (their own and others).

My nearest ancestors were an eclectic bunch of plantation workers, coal miners, immigrants, laundromat owners, burlesque dancers, American World War II vets and war survivors, grade-school principals, caregivers, all of whom still live within me.

I'm a fourth-generation Korean-American (a descendent of some of the first Koreans who came to work in American plantations), and the daughter of a Japanese mother (shin-nisei, meaning Japanese immigrants who came after the internment camps of WW2).

My parents met at school in Hawai'i, which is why I got my Hawaiian name. But I was born and raised in Los Angeles, California, and more specifically the San Gabriel Valley. I love the culture in SoCal, from the bandas that play late into the night, the unassuming strip malls with the best fusion cuisines, even to the squawking bright green parrots on the powerlines.

Kinesthetic: I love to move my body by any means possible, but I'm particularly enamored by mobility and flexibility... I love stretching & yoga, martial arts like muay thai, the occasional bouldering session, and dancing to afrobeats, dancehall, hip-hop or amapiano.

Mental: To move my mind, I read lots of fiction (I think fiction is more “real” than nonfiction). I like the pretentious stuff that plays with language in crazy ways–so lots of modernism, but really anything written in 20th century or in America. My favorite writers though, for some reason, write in Romance languages: Clarice Lispector (Brazil), Fernando Pessoa (Portugal), and Borges (Argentina).

Auditory: I find joy in a huge variety of genres, but my favorites are 70s soul and funk, RnB, hip hop, 60s folk revival, Hawaiian, and latin indie.
