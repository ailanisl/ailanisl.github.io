## As cultural technologies (Excerpts from "Large AI models are cultural and social technologies" by Farrell, Gopnik, Shalizi, Evans.)
Humans rely extensively on cultural and social technologies. These technologies are only possible, however, because humans have distinct capacities characteristic of intelligent agents. 

- humans and other animals can perceive and act on a changing external world, build new models of that world, revise those models as they accumulate more evidence, and then design novel goals.
- Individual humans can create **novel beliefs and values and convey those beliefs and values through language, print, etc., to others**.
- Cultural and social technologies transmit and organize those beliefs and values in powerful ways, but without those individual capacities, the cultural and social technologies would have no purchase. **Without innovation, there would be no point to imitation**.

### Large Models

Claims that scaling (putting more data and computing power through the same design) of Large Models will lead to AGI agents. But LMs are different from intelligent agents. Rather, they combine the features of cultural and social technologies in a new way: 

- They generate summaries of unmanageably large & complex bodies of human-generated info (like library categories, Internet search, Wiki)
- AND they reorganize/reconstruct representations of ‘simulations’ of this info at scale/novelly (like markets, states, bureaucracies)

> LMs are ‘lossy JPEGs’ of the data corpora on which they have been trained
> 

### Foisting agency on LMs

- **Because it’s hard for humans to think clearly about large-scale cultural and social technologies, we have tended to think of them in terms of agents. Stories are a particularly powerful way to pass on information, and from fireside tales to novels to video games, they have done this by creating illustrative fictional agents, even though listeners know that those agents aren’t real. …**Similarly, it’s easy to treat markets and states as if they were real agents, and agencies or companies can even have a kind of legal personhood. 

LLMs and Large Multi-modal models are statistical models that take enormous corpora of text produced by humans, break them down into particular words, and estimate the probablity distribution of long word sequences. 

- This is an imperfect representation of language but contains a surprisingly large amount of info about the patterns it summarizes

In practice, the most recent versions of these systems depend not only on massive caches of text and images generated and curated by humans but also on human judgment and knowledge in other forms 

- Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
- Prompt engineering (background knowledge and ingenuity from humans)

> The relatively simple though powerful algorithms that allow LMs to extract statistical patterns from text are not really the key to the model’s success.
> 

Instead modern AI depends on libraries, the Web, human coders, users. 

### Onwards 

- Orienting debates about LLMs and their impacts necessitates recognition of the communities between new arguments and old ones and carefully mapping the new particulars of the new and evolving technologies.

## __Generative vs Predictive AI__
__Generative AI__: AI that is used to _make_ things using existing data. 
- genuine technical advances so far

__Predictive AI__: AI that is used to _predict_ things using existing data.
- Less progress, different technology

> Predictive AI has riden the hype around Generative AI, unfairly. To make decisions based on AI predictions (in finance, education, justice system, hiring) is potentionally dangerous and will have societal ramifications. We cannot predict the future, outside some small domanins. (paraphrasing Sayash Kapoor on _Utopias_ podcast)

Data applied to people, individuals is impossible, because we do not know how to quantify human experience.
