I flew inside an aluminum craft to Detroit a few days ago. A four hour flight. My body gets supremely uncomfortable on flights, like it isn’t used to air elevations on the magnitude of tens of thousands of feet. 

<img src="/assets/img/posts/detroit.webp" alt="postcard of detroit skyline">

To take my mind off my body, my gaze is drawn to the screens diagonal to my line of sight. A woman is watching Fox News. Drawn out coverage of a white woman getting beat up, in what was labelled a racially-motivated attack. The endless loops of the phone footage plays and plays: I do not wonder why we are so desensitized to violence. It is a sensation, visceral and carnal, of addiction.

Later, she is watching *The Wedding Banquet*, a movie about Asian American gay and lesbian relationships. The duality of woman. She is hard to pin down.

I sleep. An announcement stirs me. 
<br>


> *We are having a medical emergency. Is there a doctor or a nurse on board?* 
<br>

The woman diagonal to me pauses her movie. Hesitates. She reaches up and turns on the assistance button, but stays put as flight attendants and a female passenger speed walk by. 

The noiseless commotion is happening a few rows down. But being in the middle seat, I’m blind to the situation. 

Another announcement: the same plea. 

She finally gets up and goes back. 

Ten minutes later, she is helping the woman back to her seat. She explains that she had fainted, and advises her to drink water due to the air pressure. The fainted woman was young, 25, and was diabetic. Everything settled down. 

Back to *The Wedding Banquet,* which we watch together, alone.

<hr>

Michigan was nice. The conference was stimulating, but academia is a cloistered world unto itself. I stayed in the shadows. 

<hr>

On the flight home, an old Filipino man commended me for moving seats to allow a family to be together. He lost his charger and we were semi-desperately searching for it, but it never showed up. He asked if he had snored while sleeping. He didn’t. He watched Mission Impossible. 

<img src="/assets/img/posts/jb.webp" alt="image of James Brown">

I watched *Get On Up,* a biopic of James Brown played by the inimitable Chadwick Boseman (rest in peace). I love getting shown pre-fame music stars hustle and unbind their talents. A jarring scene: young JB stealing the shoes of a lynched man. Another one: those battle royales with young black boys, surrounded by white sophisticates. Again, that theme of sensation and spectacle, which one watches from a distance. 

Young James is among them, the smallest of the group. As he lies knocked down, he watches the band of black musicians playing their white-friendly music. But in James's eyes, ear, and soul, it becomes  <a href="{{ '/articles/funk/' | relative_url }}">The Funk.</a> 

The question of whether Brown sold his soul to the White Devil is murky. In one sense, he was all about the bag, using the White Man's nasty tactics to win in the White Man's world. In the other sense, he was the Godfather of Funk, unleashing the downbeat, Blackness, and roots to the mainstream. 

Chadwick has too kind of an aura to play such a problematic and deeply troubled Brown. But he was astounding in *Ma Rainey’s Black Bottom*.

> The <a href="{{ '/articles/funk/' | relative_url }}">funk</a> is in the bag and the bag is the bass and the bass never change. It's a groove. Soon as you hear that groove, I know I got ya.
